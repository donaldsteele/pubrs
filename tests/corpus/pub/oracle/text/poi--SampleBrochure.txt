Picasso

"Vincent van Gogh was born near Brabant,
the son of a minister. In 1869, he got a
position at the art dealers, Goupil and Co. in
The Hague, through his uncle, and worked
with them until he was dismissed from the
London office in 1873. He worked as a
schoolmaster in England (1876), before
training for the ministry at Amsterdam
University (1877). After he failed to get a post
in the Church, he went to live as an
independent missionary among the Borinage
miners. Van Gogh’s preferred medium was
oil on canvas which at times he liberally
applied using a palette knife. His expressive
use of paint as illustrated in ‘Starry Night’
resulted in the uniquely recognizable trait of
suggestive movement.

The key concept of Cubism is that the essence of
objects can only be captured by showing it from
multiple points of view simultaneously.
Picasso’s work would have to be one of the best
examples of this type or artwork.
Trained in the traditional methods of art, Picasso
broke away developing his own style. The deep pain
over the death of his friend become apparent in
Picasso’s “blue period”. This was followed by his
“Rose period” in which the love he found in his soonto-be-wife blossomed both in his life and on the
pallet.
