# 0022 Tight, through and both-sides wrap

- Context: T10 (M18). Publisher wraps text around a shape's outline, through its holes, and on both sides of it. ADR 0006 chose bounding boxes and one segment per line. Locked Decisions 2 (unset fields lay out as before), 3 (no per-edit cost that grows with the document) and 4 (deterministic output) bind.
- Decision:
  - **Model.** `WrapMode` gains `tight` and `through`; `TextWrap` gains `both_sides` (default false, skipped when false). Schema 9, identity migration. `square` takes the widest gap only, bit for bit as in M17, whatever the new fields hold elsewhere.
  - **Outlines.** An obstacle with `tight` or `through` wrap carries polygons in the text frame's local space (`Outline`, compared by value with a pointer shortcut, so the story cache key covers them). Rectangles: four corners, or each rounded corner as 8 chords scaled out so they stay outside the arc. Ellipse: 64 vertices on a circumscribed ellipse (scaled by 1/cos(pi/64)). Line: its stroked quad, butt caps, half the stroke width (at least 0.25 pt). Rotated obstacles: every vertex rotated about the obstacle's centre, then through the inverse of the text frame's rotation, as the bounding box already was. Frames without an outline of their own (text, groups) and images without a usable contour use the bounding box.
  - **Margin.** Not a polygon offset: for a band `y0..y1`, the polygon is sampled over `y0 - m .. y1 + m` and each covered span is widened by `m` on both sides. That is a square offset of the outline, so the clearance is never less than the margin (up to 41% more at diagonals, on the safe side) and nothing is built per margin.
  - **Image contours.** `pubrs-layout` cannot decode images, so it defines `AlphaContours` (`contour(asset) -> Option<Arc<Contour>>`, set on the `Layouter` with `set_contours`, the way the render crate takes an `ImageResolver`). `pubrs-io`'s `ImageCache` traces each decoded raster once and shares a `ContourStore` with the layouter: alpha at least 50% (128) counts as covered; the mask is reduced to at most 256 cells on its longer side (a cell is covered when any pixel is, so the outline errs outward); the boundary between covered and uncovered cells is followed into closed loops (holes are loops too, coverage is even-odd); loops are simplified with Douglas-Peucker at a tolerance of one cell (one pixel up to 256 px, so the outline may cut up to about a pixel into the visible pixels). Fully opaque, fully clear and undecoded images have no contour and wrap by their rectangle; JPEG and SVG likewise. Layout maps the contour with `image_placement` (moved from `pubrs-render` to `pubrs-core`, so render and wrap share one implementation and the goldens are unchanged) and clips it to the visible destination rectangle. `Session::layout` and `ExportContext::layout` decode only the assets that tight or through frames use (`ImageCache::sync_for_wrap`). A contour that arrives later changes the regions, hence the cache key, hence the layout.
  - **Modes per line band.** `tight` blocks the hull `[min x, max x]` of an obstacle's covered spans; `through` blocks the spans themselves, so text enters notches and holes. The covered spans are exact per sub-band: between consecutive vertex heights the crossing edges keep their order, so they pair up into spans and each span is taken at its widest over the sub-band (conservative for slanted edges). `square` and `top_bottom` are unchanged. When nothing fits, `tight` and `through` obstacles resume one point lower (their gaps can reopen before the bounding box ends); the others skip to the box's bottom as before.
  - **Slots.** A band's free space is the line minus the blocked spans. Without `both_sides`, the widest gap is used (today's rule), unless an obstacle is `through`. With `both_sides` on any obstacle in the band, or `through`, every gap at least three em wide (the minimum a line already needed: `max(3 x font size, 18 pt)`) becomes a slot, left to right.
  - **Flow.** A row with several slots breaks one parley line into each slot, left to right, at the same y, then moves down by the tallest. Each slot's line is its own `LineBox` (own x and width, carets, selection and hit boxes) in story order, slot by slot. Because parley can only undo the last line, a multi-slot row keeps a checkpoint of the breaker state, the hyphenation cursor and the placement lists; the taller-line retry compares the whole slot list for the taller band and restores the checkpoint, a line that would pass the region's bottom sends the whole row to the next region. A line ending at a frame or column break ends the row; a soft line break moves on to the next slot. Single-slot rows take exactly the old path (`revert`, one placement), so existing layouts are bit-identical.
  - **Cost.** Polygons are built only for frames with tight or through wrap, in `regions_for` (the same function the cache key uses). The scan per line costs edges x sub-bands for the obstacles in the band only. A frame set to square pays nothing new; the 50- and 200-page perf documents use square, so they show only the overhead of the extra fields and checks (see PROGRESS.md).
- Consequence:
  - Rows with several slots align lines on the same top, so baselines agree when the lines have the same metrics (the usual case); mixed sizes in one row leave their baselines apart.
  - A word wider than its slot overflows it (parley does not break inside a word that fits no line), so a three-em slot can show a long word poking out.
  - Widow and orphan fixes, drop-cap placement and keep rewinds look at the first slot of a row; a widow break that would fall between two slots of a row takes effect at the next row.
  - Obstacle polygons inside groups, and WordArt frames, wrap by their bounding box.
