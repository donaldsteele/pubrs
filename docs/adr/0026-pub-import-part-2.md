# 0026 Publisher (`.pub`) import, part 2: pictures, shapes, colours, tables, masters and entry points

- Context: ADR 0017 covers the container, pages and text (T23, M31). T24 (M32) adds what the first part reported as dropped: pictures, shapes, colour schemes, tables and masters, plus the way users reach the importer (MCP, CLI, GUI, file associations). Format facts below were read off the 28-file corpus; libmspub and POI HPBF remain references only (no code taken).
- Decision:
  - **Pictures.**
    - Where they live: `Escher/EscherStm` starts with a drawing-group container (`0xF000`) holding a BLIP store container (`0xF001`) of file BLIP store entries (FBSE, `0xF007`, 36-byte header). In every corpus file the FBSE has no embedded picture; its `foDelay` field is the offset of a BLIP record (`0xF018..=0xF117`) in `Escher/EscherDelayStm`. An FBSE with an embedded BLIP (record longer than 36 plus the name) is read too, but no corpus file has one. Only `51318`, `SampleBrochure` and `SampleNewsletter` carry pictures (9, 8 and 1 BLIPs).
    - A picture shape names its BLIP by Escher property `0x4104` (pib, 1-based; the `0x4000` bit is the "blip" flag of the property id). The importer reads `0x0104` too.
    - Formats found: JPEG (`0xF01D`), PNG (`0xF01E`) and WMF (`0xF01B`). The two "PNG" BLIPs of `SampleBrochure` hold GIF data (`GIF89a`), so the format is sniffed from the bytes. PNG and JPEG go through `import_image` unchanged; a GIF is decoded (first frame, at most 16 Mpx, header size checked first) and stored as PNG. That needed the `image` crate's `gif` feature (`gif`, `weezl` and `color_quant` were already in `Cargo.lock` through other crates).
    - **WMF and EMF: no extractor.** The plan allowed a hand-written bitmap extractor only if an oracle image was a metafile. None is: the oracle's 7 images are 1 PNG (`51318`), 1 JPEG (`SampleNewsletter`) and 2 GIFs + 1 JPEG (`SampleBrochure`, which `pdfimages` lists as 5 because the GIFs are transparent and each soft mask is listed as an image of its own). The 13 WMFs in the corpus were inflated and walked in a scratch tool: all hold vector records only (polygons, polylines, brushes), no `META_STRETCHDIB` or `META_DIBSTRETCHBLT`. So no inflate or metafile parser is in the importer. A WMF or EMF picture becomes an empty picture frame at the shape's place (the user can fill it) and is reported as `picture-wmf` or `picture-emf` with a count; PICT, DIB and TIFF as `picture`. A WMF renderer is a TODO.
    - Placement: the image frame stretches the picture to the shape's rectangle (Escher crop properties are not read), keeps rotation, and has no text wrap (the file's text boxes overlap pictures on purpose). A BLIP used by several shapes becomes one asset. A pib of 0 or past the store is reported as `picture`.
    - 98 and 2000 files: no corpus file has a picture in those versions (their `EscherStm` is empty and the shape markers of picture shapes are not known), so pictures there stay reported as dropped shapes. Nothing is guessed.
  - **Shapes.** Escher shape types 1 (rectangle), 2 (rounded rectangle, radius a sixth of the short side), 3 (ellipse) and 20 (line; direction from the FSP flip flags: exactly one flip means up-right) become shape frames; any other type is reported as `shape` with the type in the detail (the corpus has types 6 and 136 in `SampleNewsletter`: right-edge strips and a separator, about 10 shapes). Group chunks (0x30) only hold other shapes and are skipped; the logo chunk (0x31) is reported.
    - Fill and outline are Escher properties, and the boolean words carry a "use" half: fill colour `0x0181`, fill booleans `0x01bf` (`fFilled` is bit 4, 0x10, "use" at 0x10_0000), outline colour `0x01c0`, width `0x01cb` (EMU), line booleans `0x01ff` (`fLine` is bit 3, 0x8, "use" at 0x8_0000). Primary and tertiary property records are merged (a "use" bit in the later one overrides). A shape is filled or outlined only when the file says so explicitly. This was checked on the corpus: the sidebar and magenta boxes of `SampleNewsletter` and the bars of `SampleBrochure` come out filled, the text boxes with a bare "use" bit do not, lines are drawn only with the line flag. The fill and outline also apply to text frames.
    - Unreadable colour (`0xffffffff`) is no colour. Width 0 is a 0.25 pt hairline.
  - **Colour schemes become swatches.** The file's palette (2002+ chunk 0x5c, 98/2000 marker 0x47) has 8 entries. The corpus has three distinct schemes (`SampleNewsletter`, `SampleBrochure`, the default one in `Sample*`); in each, entries 0 to 6 read as Main (text), four accents, Hyperlink and Followed Hyperlink, and entry 7 is white. Entries 0 to 6 therefore become the seven `SchemeSlot` swatches ("Scheme: Accent 1" and so on, fixed ids, via `AddSwatch`) with the file's colours; entry 7 becomes a swatch "Scheme: Background". The document's `scheme` id stays unset (it names a built-in scheme). Colour references of type `0x08` (palette index; in text colour runs and in Escher fill and line colours) become `Paint::Swatch` with no tint; any other value is a direct `0x00BBGGRR` colour. The mapping of 0 to 6 to slots is inferred from the order and the look of these three schemes, not documented anywhere.
  - **Tables.** The corpus has one: `Sample*.pub` page 2 (eight files, five generations of the same sample) holds a 3 by 2 table. The shape chunk has type `0x10` (Escher type 201); block `0x66` is the row count, `0x67` the column count, `0x6d` a directory whose first `cols` entries give column end positions and the next `rows` entries row end positions (EMU from the table's origin; id 1 of each entry). A child chunk of type `0x63` lists the cells (block `0x02`; per cell ids 1 and 2 are the first and last row, 3 and 4 the first and last column, 0 when absent) in row-major order, and the table's whole text is one story with one paragraph per cell, also row-major. The importer builds a table frame (`TableFrame::grid`, `AddTable`, one story per cell) when the cells are exactly the rows by columns grid with no merges and the story has exactly one paragraph per cell; widths and heights come from the end positions. Anything else (merged cells, other paragraph counts, odd positions, a total over 14,400 pt) keeps the text as a text frame and is reported as `table` with the reason. Merged cells and cell borders and fills are therefore not imported (not in the corpus, so not guessed). Cell insets are 2.88 pt, as for text frames.
  - **Masters.** Every 2002+ page chunk carries a master reference in block `0x0d` (type `0x68`, the seq of a page chunk); in the whole corpus every real page names the same chunk (seq 0x107) and that chunk lists no shape. The importer creates a `MasterPage` for each referenced chunk (named "Master", or "Master N" if there are several), assigns it to the pages that name it (`SetPageMaster`), and puts the master's listed shapes on it through the same frame pipeline as pages (`Parent::Master`). No corpus master has a shape, so the master-with-frames path is not exercised by a corpus file (it has no separate test beyond the shared pipeline). A reference to a missing chunk is dropped silently (the page simply has no master); a reference to another page of the document is dropped and reported as `master`. Publisher 98 and 2000 files have no such reference in the corpus; their placeholder pages stay dropped as before.
  - **Fonts.** The document keeps the file's family names, so an installed font is used. `ImportReport.fonts` lists the families used. `pubfile::note_missing_fonts` (called by `doc_import`, which has the session's `FontLibrary`) adds a `font-fallback` entry naming each family the library lacks, with "X is not installed; Liberation Sans is used" (`FALLBACK_SANS`; layout sets Latin script fallback to it). The CLI and the GUI go through `doc_import`.
  - **Report.** `ImportReport` gains `pictures`, `shapes`, `tables`, `masters`, `swatches`, `fonts` and a `summary()` line ("Imported 4 pages, 82 frames, 1 picture, ...; dropped: ...; 7 fonts substituted"). New kinds: `picture`, `picture-wmf`, `picture-emf`, `shape`, `table`, `master`, `font-fallback`. Every shape that is not imported is counted; the existing kinds and the budget stay. The report derives `JsonSchema` for MCP.
  - **Entry points.**
    - MCP `doc_import {path}` returns `{doc_id, title, page_ids, revision, report, summary}`. The document has no path (the first save asks for one) and its title is the file name.
    - CLI `pubrs import <file.pub> [-o out.pubrs] [--json]` prints the summary (or `{output, report}` as JSON) and writes the `.pubrs`; an explicit `-o` overwrites, the default (next to the input) refuses to.
    - GUI: File, "Import Publisher File…" (no shortcut), `FileDialogs::pick_publisher_file`, a report dialog (summary plus a scroll list of kind and count with detail), drag and drop of `.pub` onto the window, and `.pub` on the command line.
    - Packaging: the Linux desktop entry lists `application/x-mspublisher` (and the Flatpak metainfo has the same `mediatype`); macOS gets a Viewer, Alternate-rank document type with an imported type declaration for extension `pub`; Windows gets `HKCR\.pub\OpenWithProgids` plus an open verb on a second ProgId, **not** an `Extension` element, so pubrs is offered under "Open with" and never becomes the default for `.pub` (Publisher keeps it). On macOS files arrive as command-line paths, exactly as `.pubrs` files do today (the app has no Apple-event open handler for either; a TODO). The macOS and Windows entries are not built or run here (CI and the release workflow build them).
  - **Robustness.**
    - BLIP reads: a BLIP must lie inside its stream; each distinct BLIP (stream, offset) is decoded once and every later FBSE or shape that points at it shares it (`Blip::Same`, one asset), so 10,000 FBSEs at one 8 MB picture cost one copy; each decode charges its whole body length to the work budget, and at most 256 MiB of picture bytes are kept (more is reported as `picture`, "blip byte budget exceeded"); a GIF's header size is checked (16 Mpx, and explicit `image::Limits`: 8192 per side, 128 MiB allocation) before decoding; a PNG or JPEG goes through `image_info`/`MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` when the asset is made (a dimension bomb becomes a reported `picture`); the BLIP count is capped (10,000); table blocks are capped (240 lines, 57,600 cells).
    - The fuzz layers now include the delay stream (mutations and truncations), and crafted tests cover: FBSE offsets, sizes and name lengths that lie, BLIP headers claiming 4 GiB, a delay stream of garbage or none, pib values 0, 9, 100,000 and `0xffffffff`, a GIF header of 65535 by 65535, a PNG header of 100,000 by 100,000, a WMF with a garbage compressed body, extreme fill, line and picture properties, and extreme table counts. `catch_unwind` stays. No non-test unwrap, expect or panic was added.
- Consequence:
  - **Oracle image counts.** The test asks for placed pictures (`FrameContent::Image` with an asset) equal to the oracle's `images`, except `SampleBrochure`, where the oracle's 5 are 3 pictures and 2 soft masks (see above), so 3 are expected. This is the one place the test does not use the raw `expected.json` number, and the reason is in the test.
  - Import then PDF export gives the oracle page count for all 22 oracle-ok files. The three goldens (`pub_sample_newsletter_p1`, `pub_sample_brochure_p1`, `pub_sample2000_p1`, 72 dpi, test fonts only) are of our own output.
  - Word recall is unchanged or better: the table now holds the `Sample*` cell text, and the test's frame text counts table cells (all `Sample*` files 1.000; `51318` 0.917, `SampleBrochure` 0.998, `SampleNewsletter` 0.955 per frame and 0.901 distinct).
